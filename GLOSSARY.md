# Personal Finance Tracking

This context describes statement activity, spending, and categorization for a personal monthly finance tracker.

## Statement and transaction language

**Financial account**:
A bank account or credit-card account owned by you.

**Statement**:
A financial account's dated record of reported activity, such as an HSBC bank statement or an SBI credit-card statement.

**Statement entry**:
A single row on a statement. One transaction usually has one statement entry, while a transfer between two of your accounts can have an entry on each account's statement.

**Transaction**:
A financial event involving money entering, leaving, or moving between your accounts. A transaction is distinct from a statement entry because one transfer can be represented by entries on two statements.

**Counterparty**:
The person or organization on the other side of a transaction.

**Payer**:
The counterparty who sends money to you.

**Payee**:
The counterparty who receives money from you.

**Merchant**:
A business counterparty. A person who pays you back remains a counterparty, not a merchant.

## Transaction kinds and spending

**Expense**:
Money spent on goods or services. A credit-card purchase is an expense; paying the credit-card bill is not.

**Income**:
Money received that is not a refund, reimbursement, or transfer between your own accounts. Salary is income.

**Transfer**:
Money moved between accounts you own. It is neither income nor an expense, even when the two sides appear as separate statement entries.

**Credit-card settlement**:
A payment from a bank account toward a credit-card balance. It is recorded as a money movement, but is excluded from expense totals because the card statement records the underlying purchases as expenses.

**Refund**:
Money returned by a merchant for a prior purchase. It offsets the relevant expense category in the month the refund arrives.

**Reimbursement**:
Money received from another person to repay part of a shared expense. It offsets the relevant expense category in the month it arrives; person-to-person incoming payments require review.

**Category**:
A label for the purpose of an expense, such as Food or Transportation. Categories may combine related spending, such as Household & Groceries, to avoid unnecessary splitting.

**Allocation**:
The portion of a transaction assigned to a category. A transaction normally uses one category; it may be split into multiple allocations when useful.

**Gross spending**:
The total of expenses before refunds and reimbursements are subtracted.

**Spending offset**:
A refund or reimbursement assigned to an expense category in the month it arrives.

**Net spending**:
Gross spending minus spending offsets for the reporting period.

## Categorization language

**Categorization rule**:
A reusable, user-confirmed instruction that matches transaction details to a category. A manual correction becomes a rule only when you explicitly confirm the reusable match.

**Categorization suggestion**:
A proposed category for a transaction that still requires review. Refunds and person-to-person incoming payments require review, even when a category can be suggested.

**Manual correction**:
A category or classification chosen by you for one transaction. It does not automatically change other transactions unless you confirm a categorization rule.
